# Design a rover

Open `/design/` or choose **Design a rover** in the app sidebar or Workshop. The normal account/demo gate applies. The guided workflow runs locally and needs no new model provider.

1. **Describe:** name the rover, describe its purpose, choose differential or mecanum drive, a starting size and sensors. Optional budget and payload targets are retained as requirements, not automatically certified. Generate an interactive preview; it creates no Workshop robot until you save.
2. **Choose parts:** replace supported roles with catalogue components. Review quantities, recorded specifications, source links, missing facts and engineering findings. Selections do not mark inventory as owned.
3. **Assemble:** save the plan, then move/rotate individual instances and plan connections in the existing workbench. Applied edits append robot versions. The effective BOM follows additions/removals; missing required drive/control/power hardware blocks consistent package export.
4. **Generate CAD:** review chassis length/width/thickness, enter any measured hole coordinates, optionally choose a component mount and electronics enclosure. Hole coordinates are millimetres relative to each plate centre. Blank means no holes. Save the CAD settings with the robot version. Oversized parts need a suitable printer or redesign; joined tiles are not generated.
5. **Build package:** download one ZIP containing the exact saved spec, BOM JSON/CSV, assembly poses, planned wiring, assembly guidance, findings, manifest, editable OpenSCAD and supported plate STL files. Enclosure box and lid are OpenSCAD, not STL. The manifest identifies the robot and assembly revision.

A local draft recovery slot preserves unfinished edits across navigation/reload. A pending draft takes precedence over a deep link to another saved rover, with a notice; save or discard it before choosing another design. Demo draft data uses a separate storage key.

## Limits

Catalogue bodies are published or explicitly illustrative envelopes, not detailed manufacturer CAD. Unknown dimensions, mass, mounting holes, pinouts and motor ratings remain missing. The design and package are PLANNED, not physically built or validated. Assembly collision/placement checks are envelope heuristics, not detailed mechanical, thermal or electrical certification.

Generated custom mounts/enclosures are design artifacts. They require physical fit checks and must be incorporated into the actual assembly and hardware parts list before construction. Existing printed parts in a concept BOM may still require their own CAD or sourced designs; descriptive Forge print-plan summaries are not exported as executable CAD.

`simulation-spec.json` is the saved RobotForge JSON for the current app. No URDF file, complete Gazebo assembly integration, real robot driving result or acceptance verdict is generated by this export.

The initial complete browser trial used a synthetic sample rover in demo storage. It verified a motor replacement, an IMU move, CAD edits with holes and enclosure, recovery after navigation, a save from another step, and an 18-file downloaded ZIP matching the saved version. This is software evidence only.
